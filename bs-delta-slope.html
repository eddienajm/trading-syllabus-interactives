THE TREE BECOMES A BELL CURVE

Left: the end-prices on an ordinary price axis. The right tail is long, so the mean (100) is dragged above the median. Right: the same bars on a log axis. Symmetric. The curve over the bars is the lognormal. Raise n and the bars hug the curve. The tilt of p above 50% is what carries the drift: p ≈ ½ + (r − σ²/2)√Δt / 2σ. It shrinks as the steps get smaller (53.8% with 4 steps, about 51% with 60) because there are more steps to accumulate the same drift.

THE LADDER OF "AT THE MONEY" STRIKES

A zoomed ruler in log price, marked in standard deviations. The median, the forward and the 50 delta strike are evenly spaced, each σ²T/2 apart in logs. At ordinary volatilities they are only about a tenth of a standard deviation apart; raise σ or T and they fan out.

DELTA IS THE SLOPE OF THE CALL PRICE

Solid curve: the call's value today. Dashed hinge: what it pays at expiry. The red tangent has slope delta: near 0 when the strike is far above spot, near 1 when it is far below, about a half in between.

STRIKE ↔ MONEYNESS ↔ DELTA

Evenly spaced strikes are not evenly spaced in moneyness, and the delta column is an S-curve: it barely moves in the wings and changes fastest near the money. Change σ and T and watch the same strikes slide to different moneyness.
